The rule that holds throughout

A key signs only after policy, approval and the institution's own Validation Gate have all said yes, and every step leaves an audit record.

Client assets sit in a DFNS vault: one balance sheet with an address per network. Controls are attached before any money arrives. Deposits wait in quarantine until someone releases them, and withdrawals need an approval. The institution's Validation Gate then gets a last veto before any key signs.

An incoming transfer never lands as spendable. DFNS books it as Quarantined. Releasing it is itself an operation under policy: it is requested with a reason, a second person approves it, and only then do the funds become Available. Send a little Sepolia ETH to the vault address to see one arrive.

The vault keeps a ledger, not just a balance. Every amount sits in exactly one state: Available to spend, Quarantined pending review, Locked for an obligation, or Outgoing while a transfer settles. Operations can see what is truly theirs to move.

A withdrawal is a request, not a transaction. DFNS reserves the amount and holds the request for approval: the requester can never approve their own. Here the checker, a service account that can only read and vote, approves small transfers to known addresses on its own. Anything else waits for a person.

The gate runs on the institution's side, so it holds even if everything upstream were bypassed.
